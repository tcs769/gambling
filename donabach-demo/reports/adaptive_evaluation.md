# Four-way evaluation and the value of judgment over time (sample household)

Generated by `python -m app.study.adaptive_evaluation`. Nothing was tuned toward a result.

* Household: the demo household (two earners aged 38 and 37; $150k brokerage, $400k pre-tax, $30k Roth, $40k cash; a home purchase in 3 years, college in 10, retirement at 62). Horizon: age 80 (42 years).
* Best fixed strategy (what the search chooses from the 4,032-strategy menu): **Hold 50% of long-term money in stocks until 58, then glide down; fill the 24% bracket with Roth conversions every year; keep a 15% private credit sleeve, in the pre-tax account first, then the Roth, then the taxable account; harvest losses at fund level.**
* Evaluation: 2,000 simulated futures the search never saw; every strategy is run on the same futures. Risk aversion 3; wealth below $50,000 counts as $50,000 in the certainty equivalent.
* The adaptive planner runs at the defaults: 32 rollouts per candidate, two coordinate-search passes, each proposed change re-tested on 64 fresh futures. It took 3.6 seconds per 100 paths on this machine's 4 cores.
* Advantages are annualized growth-rate differences (see `app/study/metrics.py`); ranges are the 5th to 95th percentile over resamples of the futures.

## Where the best fixed strategy's advantage over the good RIA comes from

The decisions switched on one at a time starting from the good RIA, cumulatively, on the same futures: what each step adds to median wealth and to certainty-equivalent wealth at 80, annualized.

| Step | Median wealth at 80 | Median advantage | Certainty equivalent | Risk-adjusted advantage |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| RIA baseline | $2,909,021 | +0.00% | $499,801 | +0.00% |
| Lower fee | $3,634,105 | +0.53% | $701,937 | +0.81% |
| Asset location | $3,794,170 | +0.63% | $712,881 | +0.85% |
| Direct indexing | $3,794,170 | +0.63% | $712,881 | +0.85% |
| Goal bonds, allocation and private credit | $2,941,276 | +0.03% | $1,420,484 | +2.52% |
| Roth timing and withdrawal order | $3,261,404 | +0.27% | $1,432,454 | +2.54% |

The lower-risk allocation and private credit step is where the risk-adjusted advantage is made, and it *costs* median wealth: the plan holds the lowest stock share on the menu and a private-credit sleeve, which lowers the middle of the distribution and, above all, the chance of running out of money (certainty-equivalent wealth is dominated by that tail).

## What judgment over time adds

The adaptive planner against the best fixed strategy, on the same futures: median +0.01%/yr (+0.00% to +0.03%) ($17,547 at 80); risk-adjusted +0.06%/yr (+0.01% to +0.17%) ($38,320 at 80).

### Where it comes from

The planner restricted to some of its decisions (the others follow the fixed strategy):

| Decisions the planner may change | Median advantage over best fixed | Risk-adjusted advantage over best fixed |
|---|---|---|
| tax timing only | +0.01% (+0.00% to +0.02%) | +0.04% (+0.01% to +0.09%) |
| allocation only | +0.00% (-0.00% to +0.01%) | +0.00% (+0.00% to +0.00%) |
| all six decisions | +0.01% (+0.00% to +0.03%) | +0.06% (+0.01% to +0.17%) |

Tax timing: Roth conversions, realizing gains in the 0% bracket, how a goal is paid, the 401(k) type. Allocation: stock share and private credit.
