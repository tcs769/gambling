Generated by `python -m app.study.reach`. This is a diagnostic: it measures how far each lever goes and adopts none of them. Nothing the app does was changed, and nothing was tuned toward a result.

**Short answer.** With the strategies on the menu (the balanced ones and the spending-reserve ones) and the market assumptions as specified, the most median wealth any plan produces is **+1.74% a year** over the good advisor (the sample household, futures the plan never saw). About +0.83% of that is Donabach's tools (fee, asset location, conversions, withdrawal order, goal funding). The other +0.90% is only that the plan holds 94% stocks against the advisor's 80% today and 95% against 50% from 70, which is more risk: it runs out of money in 1.1% of futures against the advisor's 0.6%. The app's own plan (equity first, risk aversion 3) is +1.62% ahead, runs out of money in 0.7% of futures and has a 10th percentile of $1,298,291 against the advisor's $941,909; its risk-adjusted advantage is +0.13%. At risk aversion 1 the planner picks +1.69% ahead with 0.9% running out. No choice on the menu reaches 2%.

## 4. What is not in the engine

These would add to an advantage but the engine does not compute them, so none is included and none is assumed: behavioral coaching (Vanguard's 'Advisor's Alpha' puts it at about 1.5% a year of its 'up to 3%', and says its components are not additive and the total is irregular, not annual; the estimate is contested), a dynamic spending rule (0 to 1.1% in the same table), Social Security claiming, a survivor's tax brackets, and diversifying a concentrated stock (the simulator holds the diversified market). Borrowing to hold more than 100% stocks would raise the median further, mechanically, by taking more risk; it is not among the strategies and is not advice.
